The rig

A coffee shop with a hitch

Lucy Lou's has never had a storefront and doesn't want one. Everything we serve comes out of a 14-foot trailer that tows to your lot, levels itself, and opens a window — or out of a cart that rolls through your front door when the trailer can't get there.

What actually shows up

Inside the trailer

Espresso Two-group machine with two grinders, so a latte and an americano pull at the same time instead of one behind the other.
Cold Cold brew on tap, an ice well, and a syrup rail with the full lotus lineup — the same one on the public menu, not a cut-down event version.
Water Onboard fresh water and a sealed waste tank. We don't need a spigot, a hose bib, or a drain anywhere near us.
Power Inverter generator rated quiet enough to sit near a ceremony. If you'd rather we plug in, two 20-amp circuits do it.
The window Serving window on the passenger side with an awning and string lights. It's usually where people end up standing all night.
The board Hand-lettered chalkboard menu. For weddings we letter it with your names, your date, and your signature drink.

Trailer or cart

Which one fits your venue

Same menu, same espresso, two different footprints. Tell us where you're putting us and we'll tell you which one to book — this is the first thing we ask anyway.

Book the trailer if

  • There's a level spot roughly 30 × 12 ft we can back into
  • The approach is paved, gravel, or firm grass — not soft turf
  • Your guests will come outside, to a lawn, patio, lot, or barn drive
  • You want the trailer in your photos, which most couples do

Book the cart if

  • You're indoors — ballroom, lobby, office, church hall
  • There's a standard double door and no stairs on the path
  • Parking is tight, or the venue won't allow a towed unit
  • You need us set up in under an hour

At the window

Every drink is made in front of you

Nothing is batched into airpots and left to sit. Shots pull to order, foam gets whipped to order, and the lotus is layered where you can watch it happen. That's most of the reason people stand at the window instead of walking away with a cup.
